01

TL;DR

Build the directory and identity layer for MTG creators — not another social feed. IMDb + LinkedIn + Bandsintown, scoped to one game.

The MTG creator ecosystem is fragmented across ten-plus platforms with no canonical layer for identity, discovery, or connection to events and stores. Today, a fan who wants to find “a budget Commander YouTuber who streams in Pacific Time and shows up at MagicCons” has nowhere to go. A store running an RCQ has no way to book a creator. A mid-sized creator has nothing to send a sponsor besides a Linktree and screenshots.

ManaMap is the directory + business infrastructure that fills those gaps. Fans bring traffic; creators, stores, and event organizers pay. The product avoids feeds, posting, and moderation entirely — existing platforms remain the content layer, ManaMap is the connective tissue.

“Fans are the audience. Creators, stores, and organizers are the customers.” — core strategic insight

The four wedges, in order of priority

  1. Searchable creator directory with rich, structured metadata (format, content type, audience style, region).
  2. Cross-platform identity — one canonical profile that aggregates YouTube, Twitch, Bluesky, Moxfield, etc.
  3. Event & convention layer — appearance tracking, local discovery, store integration.
  4. Creator & store business tools — media kits, booking, sponsored placement, analytics.
02

Thesis & positioning

A vertical infrastructure play, not a social play. Niche-first; defensible through structured data + relationships, not network effects of posting.

The opportunity in one sentence

There is no canonical layer for MTG creators. Today, identity is duct-taped together with Linktrees and starter packs; discovery is community-maintained spreadsheets; bookings happen via DM. Every alternative is either a community (Discord, Reddit) or a single-feature tool (Linktree, Moxfield). None aggregate identity, none connect creators to events, none are searchable as a real database.

Why “infrastructure,” not “social”

Building another feed-driven social platform invites direct competition with YouTube, Twitch, TikTok, and Bluesky — each with thousand-engineer headstarts. Worse, social platforms require moderation, algorithmic ranking, and 24/7 engagement to retain users. ManaMap’s users come back when they want to find something. The product is a tool, not a habit loop.

Defensibility

Three compounding moats:

  1. Structured metadata. Every creator tagged by format, content type, audience, region. This data takes years to build and powers the recommendation engine.
  2. Two-sided directory. Stores list events; creators list appearances; both pull each other in. The graph is the product.
  3. SEO surface. Pages like /edh-creators, /seattle, /magiccon-vegas-2026 capture long-tail search the existing players don’t.
03

Audience & customers

Three primary user types — and the recurring reminder that the people who bring traffic aren’t the same people who pay.

~10M
MTG players globally (est.)
~2,500
Active EN-speaking creators (rough order of magnitude)

Fans — the audience (free)

Players looking for creators to follow, decks to copy, events to attend, stores to visit. They never pay. Their job is to make the directory useful — without traffic, there’s no sponsorship value.

Creators — primary customer

From 500-sub niche channels to 100k+ established voices. Free tier is table stakes; the upsell is media kits, analytics, sponsorship tools, and priority placement. Mid-tier creators (5k–50k) are the sweet spot — large enough to monetize, small enough that ManaMap meaningfully changes their business.

Stores & organizers — highest-margin customer

LGSs and event organizers are starved for marketing tools. A store page + creator-booking marketplace + boosted event listings is the SaaS package. Seattle pilots first; expand by region.

Sponsors & brands — long-tail customer

Once enough creators have media kits and stores have audiences, brands enter via marketplace fees and sponsored placement. This is Phase 4+, not now.

If a major TCG-adjacent player (Wizards, a big content network, or a deckbuilding site) pivoted into creator infrastructure, they’d be a real threat. The bet is that none of them want to own the “identity” problem — it’s low-margin until scale.

Brand mark & system

A wordmark and the smallest possible symbolic gesture — five colored dots for the color pie, no mana glyphs. Editorial, infrastructure-grade, not gamified.

ManaMap

A wordmark, a small mark (the dot), a five-color palette. Nothing else.

Wordmark

Newsreader, italic on “Map.” The italic creates a small visual lean — “moving toward.” The dot between words is the only graphic element, sized to optical center.

Marks

Three valid lockups: wordmark / wordmark + dot / dot alone (favicon, app icon, social avatar). Never use the dot bigger than the cap-height.

Color use

WUBRG colors only as data accents: pip dots on creator cards, filter chips on search. Never as decoration, never as backgrounds. The product looks like a document; color is information.

07

Product principles

Six rules to point back at when scope creeps.

1. Directory, not feed.

No timeline, no algorithmic ranking by recency, no posting surface. If the answer to a feature involves “users will see what others are doing right now,” the answer is no.

2. Structured data first.

Every creator, event, store has typed metadata. Free-text bios are augmentation, not the substance. This is what makes us defensible.

3. The product is a tool.

Users come back when they have a job. Success looks like “sub-30 second time-to-find,” not “time-on-site.”

4. Existing platforms are the content layer.

YouTube hosts the videos. Twitch hosts the streams. We deep-link, embed where it’s easy, and never replicate.

5. Fans free forever. Pros pay.

Monetization never gates discovery for fans. Creators, stores, and brands pay for amplification, tools, and analytics.

6. SEO is a first-class surface.

Every creator, tag, event, and region is a server-rendered page. A “Best Pauper YouTubers” page should outrank a Reddit thread.

Indexes that matter

The search experience lives or dies on composite indexes over (formatTag, contentTag, region), full-text on bio + displayName, and a denormalized followers_total for sort. Build search on Postgres first; Elasticsearch comes only when query latency forces it.

Roadmap

Six phases. Each is allowed to take longer than planned but never to absorb the next phase’s scope. Discovery before social before commerce — strictly.

Phase 0 · 1–3 weeks · NOW

Validation

Confirm there is real pull from creators and stores before writing meaningful code.
Tasks
  • 10–20 creator interviews
  • 3–5 LGS conversations
  • Landing page + waitlist
  • Mock profile screenshots
Deliverables
  • This design doc (v0.1)
  • Domain + handles
  • Waitlist (target: 200 emails)
  • Pain-point synthesis
Kill criteria
  • Fewer than 5 creators say they’d maintain a profile
  • Stores don’t respond to outreach
  • No clear differentiated wedge after interviews

Ship a usable creator directory. Nothing more, nothing less.

  • Responsive mobile UI

What we explicitly will not do

  • Charge fans. No paywalled directory, no fan subscriptions, no “premium” discovery.
  • Sell user data. Aggregated audience demographics in media kits are creator-opt-in; raw user data never leaves the platform.
  • Run ads against fan traffic. Display advertising trains us toward the wrong metrics.

GTM & marketing

Don’t do a big launch. Onboard quietly, build the directory under-the-radar, then announce when the data is the moat.

Channels, in order

  1. Direct creator outreach. Personal DMs to 100 creators. Hand-claim profiles. Make it feel inevitable.
  2. Reddit. r/EDH, r/magicTCG, format-specific subs. Useful posts (“we built a directory of every Pauper YouTuber under 10k”), not promo.
  3. Bluesky. Creator hashtags, event hashtags, partner with starter-pack curators.
  4. Discord. MTG creator servers + LGS communities. Quiet seeding, no spam.
  5. Conventions. Show up at MagicCon Vegas with claimed-profile QR cards for creators.
  6. Local pilot. Seattle / PNW LGS partnerships — three flagship stores deeply integrated.
  7. SEO long game. Tag pages, format pages, region pages crawled and ranked over 6–12 months.
Launch principle

Soft. Onboard 100 creators before any public post. The first impression should be “oh — there’s already everyone on here,” not “oh, a new platform with twelve profiles.”

Risks

The five things most likely to kill this.

RiskWhyMitigation
Chicken & egg Fans want creators; creators want audiences. Either side seeing an empty room walks. Seed 100–500 profiles manually before public launch. Creators don’t need to be claimed yet — directory still useful.
Drift into social Pressure to add feeds, follows, comments — “engagement” metrics encourage it. Hold the line on principles. No timeline, no posting. If a feature reads as “feed,” cut it.
Slow SEO ramp Tag/format pages may take 6–12 months to rank. Don’t depend on SEO for Phase 1. Direct outreach + creator-shared profile URLs carry early growth.
YouTube / Twitch API friction Rate limits, OAuth complexity, terms changes. Cache aggressively. Treat sync as “last successful pull,” not real-time. Fall back to handle + manual import.
LGS sales motion Selling SaaS to small local businesses is famously hard. Free Listed tier; PNW pilot to learn the motion before scaling. Lean on community goodwill, not cold sales.
WotC competition Wizards could build a creator directory tomorrow. Unlikely — different incentive (sell cards, not creator infra). But maintain credibility w/ creators so they choose us first.
